## 1. The problem & why it exists

A concurrent test passes because it sorts responses by completion and ignores call intervals. Keep histories small, include unknown outcomes explicitly, and verify the checker on impossible histories too.

## 3. Step-by-step mechanism & minimal dry-run

A linearizability test records invocation and response, preserves real-time precedence for nonoverlapping calls, and looks for a legal sequential specification. Fault injection needs a scoped model and observable oracle.

**Trace.** invoke/complete intervals → precedence graph → legal serialization search.

Try the oracle in the [coordination lab](../labs/coordination.py) with a write of 1 in interval [0,2] and a read of 0 in [1,4], then change the read interval to [3,4]. The first history has a legal read-before-write order. The second has no valid order. The oracle accepts at most eight completed operations and does not model pending outcomes.

## 4. Formal rigor & quantitative math

For four fully overlapping operations, naive search may inspect 4!=24 orders. Precedence and model pruning reduce the search.

Predict the worked quantities by hand, then run the [example](../examples/A06.32.py). It uses a small calculation or state model. For arithmetic, retain the units attached to the worked formula.
